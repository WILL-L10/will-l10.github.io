### Notable Challenges

**Hidden knob-and-tube** — discovered mid-project in walls, requiring expanded scope. Traced every circuit with a tone generator, mapped the system before removal, and coordinated with the homeowner for additional wall access without major disruption.

**No existing ground system** — metal water pipes had been replaced with PVC. Solution: installed 8-foot copper ground rods at the service entrance, bonded to the water main pre-PVC transition, verified ground resistance under 25Ω with a megger.

## Hardwood Flooring

**Total installed:** 8,000+ sq ft across 12+ residential projects.

**Materials:** 3/4" solid red oak, 5/8" engineered hickory, 1/2" engineered maple — various stains and finishes.

### Installation Process

Every job follows the same quality baseline:

```
Subfloor prep:
  ✓ Moisture content < 12%
  ✓ Flatness within 1/8" per 10 feet
  ✓ Squeaks eliminated (screws every 6")
  ✓ 15-lb felt underlayment
  ✓ 3/4" expansion gap at perimeter
```

Then: layout planning, straight chalk lines, face-nail first row, blind-nail at 45° with a pneumatic nailer, staggered joints minimum 6", cut-ins at vents and doorways with a jigsaw, and a 3-coat polyurethane finish with sanding between coats.

### Challenging Install: 2,400 sq ft Two-Story

- Material: 3/4" solid red oak, dark walnut stain
- Uneven subfloor — 1/2" dip in the living room. Fixed with tapered shims under felt paper, verified with a 6-foot straightedge.
- Six floor vents requiring precision cutouts — made cardboard templates, used a router with a flush-trim bit for clean edges, fabricated matching oak vent covers.
- Complex staircase transition — scribed boards to the stair nose, matched hardwood treads to the field, quarter-round for a seamless transition.

## Bathroom Renovations

**Count:** 8 complete gut renovations.

### Full Scope Example

**Before:** Single vanity, fiberglass tub/shower combo, vinyl floor, builder lighting.

**After:** Double vanity with quartz countertop, custom tile shower with glass enclosure, 12×24 porcelain floor tile, recessed LED lighting.

### Phase 1 — Demo & Rough-In

Two days of full tearout: vanity, toilet, fixtures, tub, flooring down to subfloor, drywall to studs. Then rough-in: PEX supply lines, relocated drain for double vanity, 20A GFCI outlet circuit, 15A lighting circuit, 110 CFM exhaust fan duct.

### Phase 2 — Waterproofing & Tile

Shower waterproofing stack:
```
1. 1/2" cement board (HardieBacker)
2. RedGard liquid membrane (2 coats)
3. Kerdi fabric at seams
4. Thinset mortar bed
5. Porcelain tile
6. Grout + sealer
```

**Tile specs:**

| Location | Material | Size | Grout |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shower walls | Porcelain subway | 3"×6" | White sanded |
| Shower floor | Porcelain hex | 2" hex | White sanded |
| Main floor | Porcelain plank | 12"×24" | Gray sanded |
| Accent wall | Glass mosaic | 1"×1" | White unsanded |

### Common Challenges

**Out-of-square walls** — adjusted tile layout to hide a 3/8" discrepancy over 8 feet by starting from center, working outward, and grading grout line width.

**Floor slope to linear drain** — built a mortar bed with 1/4" per foot pitch using screed guides, verified with a level and straightedge before waterproofing.

**Vanity drain misalignment** — offset with 45° fittings accessed through a cut panel in the vanity back, verified proper venting before close-up.

## Why This Matters for Engineering

The parallel isn't forced. A few concrete examples:

- **Load calculations** for wire sizing are applied circuit analysis — Ohm's law, NEC derating factors, 80% continuous load rules.
- **Systematic fault tracing** in electrical (isolate, test, eliminate) is identical to hardware debugging methodology.
- **Waterproofing layer stacks** require the same systems-level thinking as interface design — get one layer wrong and everything above it fails.
- **Reading plans and adapting to field conditions** is what embedded engineers do when hardware doesn't match spec.

I don't just know how circuits work on paper. I've installed them, tested them, and had them inspected.
